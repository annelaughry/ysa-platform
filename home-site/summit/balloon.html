Payload

What's flying

The payload box carries a student-built electronics stack designed and assembled by Stealth and Supernovas over months of engineering work. Every component was selected, tested, and integrated by the teams — from the pressure-rated housing to the parachute recovery system.

Supernovas' VISION mission links the near-space atmospheric dataset to their ongoing study of orbital debris patterns, making this flight scientifically valuable well beyond the summit itself.

Launch Day

July 12 timeline

All times EEST. Meet at the lake field — watch for Summit staff with the orange flag.

7:00 AM
Payload check & balloon inflation
Teams gather at the lake field. Final sensor checks, battery loads, and balloon fill with hydrogen or helium under Akram's checklist.
9:00 AM
🎈 LAUNCH — Balloon and payload released
Lift-off. The balloon enters the stratosphere over roughly 90 minutes. Live tracker goes active.
9:30 AM
Recovery mission begins
Tracking signal active. Recovery crew follows predicted landing path using the SPOT tracker data.
~10:30 AM
Payload lands — recovery crew retrieves it
Balloon bursts at peak altitude. Parachute deploys, payload descends into the Codrii Reserve area for retrieval.
Afternoon
Data download & analysis
Payload returned to Reserve HQ. Raw sensor data downloaded, and Supernovas begins the first pass of atmospheric analysis.

Science Objectives

What we're measuring

Three core datasets the payload captures from ground to stratosphere and back.

🌡️
Atmospheric Temperature
Temperature gradient from ground level to stratosphere — capturing the dramatic drop through the troposphere and the inversion layer above.
📊
Barometric Pressure
Pressure drop from 101 kPa at sea level to approximately 1 kPa at 32 km altitude — a 100x reduction that validates our sensor stack's range.
🛰️
GPS Flight Path
Real-time latitude, longitude, altitude, and maximum height reached — producing a full 3D trajectory log of the entire flight.
